Thesis:
Adding directed friendship preferences changes course allocation from an independent preference assignment into a socially coupled combinatorial assignment problem; the main contribution is a deterministic HBS-style mechanism that makes this coupling explicit, measurable, and experimentally improvable after the draft.

Audience:
Professors and students in an academic seminar, familiar with algorithms, optimization, or operations research but not necessarily with this implementation.

One-line arc:
Define the classical allocation gap, introduce friendship-aware utility, show the mechanism and post-allocation search, then use experiments to explain the welfare-social-fairness trade-off.
