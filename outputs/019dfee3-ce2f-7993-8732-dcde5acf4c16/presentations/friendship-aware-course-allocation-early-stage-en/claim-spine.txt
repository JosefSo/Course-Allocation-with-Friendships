Thesis:
This project is at an early stage and explores how friendship relations can be represented inside course allocation as a formal social preference term.

Audience:
Academic seminar with professors and students.

One-line arc:
Start with the intuition, show how friendships become mathematical inputs, describe the prototype, and end with what will be evaluated next.

Slide list:
01. This is early-stage research on adding friendship relations to course allocation.
02. The motivation is simple: students may care about both courses and classmates.
03. The research question is how to add social preferences without leaving the allocation framework.
04. The core modeling step is translating "I want to be with this friend in this course" into a course-specific social value.
05. The utility model combines course value and friendship value through a lambda weight.
06. The prototype already connects inputs, allocation, logs, and metrics.
07. Preliminary runs are used to test the pipeline, not to claim final results.
08. Next steps are model refinement, better data, and careful evaluation.
