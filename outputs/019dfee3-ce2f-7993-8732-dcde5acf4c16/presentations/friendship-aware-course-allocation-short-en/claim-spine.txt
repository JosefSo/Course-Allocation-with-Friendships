Thesis:
Friendship-aware course allocation extends classical course assignment by making directed social preferences part of the utility model and then evaluating how different post-allocation improvement methods affect utility and fairness.

Audience:
Academic seminar with professors and students.

One-line arc:
Define the gap, formalize the social utility, explain the mechanism, then show that hybrid-global maximizes average utility while drop-add-global is best on average total Gini.

Slide list:
01. The project adds friendship as a formal allocation variable.
02. The research gap is independence: classical course allocation misses social externalities.
03. The model combines rankings, directed friendship preferences, and lambda weights.
04. The mechanism has two stages: reactive snake draft and post-allocation local search.
05. The experiment compares six modes across 180 runs.
06. Hybrid-global is the clear average-utility winner.
07. Fairness tells a different story: drop-add-global has the lowest average total Gini.
08. The main trade-off is utility versus fairness and base-preference inequality.
09. The contribution is a reproducible framework for studying social preferences in course allocation.
