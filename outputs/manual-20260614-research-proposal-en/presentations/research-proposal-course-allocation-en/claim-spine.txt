Thesis:
Course allocation misses an important social factor when it assumes independent student preferences. This research proposal extends an HBS-style snake draft with directed friendship preferences, measures the effect on welfare, fairness, and social satisfaction, and tests whether post-allocation local search improves the resulting allocation.

Audience:
Academic seminar audience in management, information systems, industrial engineering and management, fair allocation, and decision mechanisms.

One-line arc:
From phenomenon and research gap, through theory and hypotheses, into a computational empirical design that measures the trade-off between social utility, individual ranking satisfaction, and fairness.
